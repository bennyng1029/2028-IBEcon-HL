# Unit 1–2 diagram coverage audit

Status: planning specification, 14 November 2026. The frozen population is the 40 `omitted` coverage IDs in `assets/js/content-en.js`, matched to `docs/coverage-checklist.json`. Common means SL and HL; HL means HL extension only. This document does not claim the diagrams have been implemented or validated. The October 2022 guide is a third-party-hosted copy; check the school's current Programme Resource Centre version. Existing twelve lesson summaries need not change merely to claim coverage.

## Presentation and acceptance contract

Use an original, guided **predict → build layers → compare → explain** sequence for every ID: predict the outcome, reveal or add correctly labelled layers with native keyboard-operable buttons, compare the original and changed state, then answer an original explanation or quantitative question with meaningful feedback. Provide visible labels as well as colour, accessible names, keyboard focus, and an equivalent text description of curves, values, arrows and shaded areas. Offer reset and reveal; avoid relying on a large free-form simulation. Values and shape checks below are independently testable acceptance examples, not mandatory real-world facts. Each topic requires its actual visible diagram, labels, construction practice and feedback; text or a relabelled unrelated graph alone cannot close an omission. Explain assumptions, units and time periods. Ensure common and HL material can be distinguished without blocking common material.

Graphify query `curriculum diagram coverage omitted topics` found the app renderer but did not expose the frozen content or geometry; exact ID and named-file inspection was therefore necessary. Six existing families are `ppc`, `market`, `policy`, `ped`, `externality`, and `firm` in `assets/js/models.js`. Reuse their suitable ideas and shared rendering where correct, not their formulas indiscriminately: PPC is linear only; market/policy are linear; PED is an elasticity calculator; externality is one negative-production cost case; firm is TR/TC, not marginal curves. Circular flows, Engel curves, several elasticity geometries and firm marginal diagrams need actual new variants; do not call a total-revenue plot an MR diagram. The following acceptance descriptions are per ID; shared examples may be reused only when all named features are present.

## Foundations: production and flows (common)

- **1.1.ppc_choice_diagram** — PPC with output X/Y axes, frontier, interior A, frontier B/C and unattainable D. Show scarcity, choice, productive efficiency and unemployment; opportunity cost is output Y forgone moving B→C. Build points after predicting feasibility; for X+Y=10 use A=(2,4), B=(2,8), C=(5,5), D=(7,6): B→C costs 3 Y; A is feasible interior, D outside.
- **1.1.ppc_growth_diagram** — Same axes and old/new frontiers; actual growth moves interior to frontier, capacity growth shifts frontier outward. Predict then layer arrows and compare: (2,4)→(2,8) on X+Y=10 leaves capacity unchanged; X+Y=12 makes (2,10) feasible and shifts both intercepts outward.
- **1.1.ppc_cost_diagram** — Compare straight and bowed-out frontiers with equal X increments and Y sacrifice marked. Build points and calculate: straight (0,10),(1,8),(2,6) has costs 2,2; bowed (0,10),(1,9),(2,6) has costs 1,3. Draw a smooth concave-to-origin frontier through suitable points, not a claim that every economy has increasing costs.
- **1.1.circular_flow_diagram** — Household, firm, finance, government and foreign-sector nodes, real factor/product arrows opposite corresponding money arrows, and labelled saving/tax/import leakages and investment/government spending/export injections. Construct from two-sector core to five sectors; trace a wage then household spending. Direction check: households → firms factors and expenditure; firms → households income and goods; domestic → finance/government/foreign is S/T/M, reverse is I/G/X. Flows are illustrations, not necessarily equal individually.

## Market foundations (common)

- **2.1.curve_diagram** — P vertical, Q per period horizontal, downward D, two points with guides. Build schedule (Q,P)=(2,8),(6,4); predict price fall from 8 to 4 and identify Qd rise 2→6, ceteris paribus.
- **2.1.shifts_diagram** — D0/D1 and P/Q axes, movement arrows along D0 versus shift at fixed price. With D0: P=10−Q, D1: P=12−Q, at P=6 quantities 4→6; a fall in own price along D0 gives 4→6 at P=4. Attribute shift to an explicit non-price determinant.
- **2.2.curve_diagram** — P/Q axes, upward S and two guides; (Q,P)=(2,2),(6,6). Predict higher own price increases quantity supplied, ceteris paribus; construct and read both points.
- **2.2.shifts_diagram** — S0/S1, distinguish own-price movement and shift at fixed price. For S0: P=Q, S1: P=Q−2, P=4 gives Q 4→6; along S0 a price rise 4→6 gives Q 4→6. Name the non-price determinant and direction.
- **2.3.equilibrium_diagram** — D, S, E, P*, Q*, axes and guides; build crossing from P=10−Q and P=Q; verify E=(5,5), Qd=Qs=5.
- **2.3.changes_diagram** — Base market plus demand and supply shift scenarios, disequilibrium price and adjustment arrows. With prior equations at P=4, shortage is 6−4=2; at P=6 surplus is 6−4=2. D increases to P=12−Q: E=(6,6); S shifts to P=Q−2: E=(6,4). Explain signalling, incentives and rationing conditional on flexible prices.
- **2.3.surplus_diagram** — D, S, equilibrium, distinct CS and PS triangles and their sum. Build boundaries then calculate with P=10−Q, P=Q: CS=PS=½×5×5=12.5, social surplus=25 monetary units per period. This simple no-externality case equates private and social values; don't shade whole rectangles as surplus.

## Elasticity (common unless marked HL)

- **2.5.ped_diagrams** — P/Q axes with relatively elastic and inelastic curves compared at a common point and scale, horizontal perfect elastic, vertical perfect inelastic, and constant unit elastic hyperbola P=12/Q. Predict response then build each curve; at Q=2,4,6 hyperbola prices are 6,3,2 and PQ=12. Label PED magnitude; a straight downward line is unit elastic at only one point, not throughout. Compare elasticity at the common point rather than assert slope alone determines elasticity across unrelated points.
- **2.5.ped_line_hl** — HL. Straight D: P=10−Q; label upper elastic, midpoint unit, lower inelastic segments. Reveal point elasticity |PED|=P/Q: at Q=2,5,8 it is 4,1,0.25. Predict which segment has greater responsiveness and explain changing ratio despite constant slope.
- **2.5.ped_revenue_diagram** — D and P×Q rectangles before/after price changes, compare elastic and inelastic segments and unit case. On P=10−Q, (Q,P)=(2,8)→(3,7) gives TR 16→21; (7,3)→(8,2) gives 21→16; unit point (5,5) maximizes TR=25. Ask which rectangle changes and why; unit point implies local, not generally unchanged revenue for arbitrary finite moves.
- **2.5.yed_engel_diagram** — Income vertical and quantity horizontal, clearly name convention, three labelled Engel curves for normal income-elastic, normal income-inelastic and inferior ranges. Construct from income–quantity pairs, compare percentage changes: income 100→120 with Q 10→14 gives YED 2; 10→11 gives 0.5; 10→9 gives −0.5 (base-period convention). Do not infer elasticity from slope alone; restrict curves to positive-income illustrated range.
- **2.6.pes_diagrams** — P/Q axes: relative elastic/inelastic at common point and scale, horizontal perfectly elastic, vertical perfectly inelastic, and unit elastic straight ray from origin P=Q. Build and compare: (Q,P)=(2,2)→(4,4) has PES=1 under base or midpoint percentage method; horizontal/vertical represent limiting cases. Explain time and other assumptions rather than ranking slopes at different reference points.

## Intervention and welfare (common)

Use the market P=10−Q, P=Q unless stated; show actual transactions and clearly name who pays/receives. Simple areas assume competitive markets, full compliance, no administrative costs and efficient allocation of traded units; controls alone need not guarantee the latter.

- **2.7.ceiling_diagram** — D/S, E, binding ceiling P=4, Qs=4, Qd=6, shortage=2. Predict then construct and explain consumers who obtain goods, suppliers and non-price rationing; note some consumers cannot buy and shortage allocation is unspecified. Do not promise every consumer gains.
- **2.7.floor_diagram** — D/S, E, binding floor P=6, Qd=4, Qs=6, surplus=2. Trace actual private sales Q=4 absent government purchase; compare consumer and producer outcomes, and distinguish possible public purchase/storage as an explicitly different scenario.
- **2.7.tax_diagram** — Specific tax t=2 shifts supply to P=Q+2; show buyers' price 6, sellers' net price 4, Q=4, vertical wedge 2 and government revenue 8. Construct shift and revenue rectangle, compare incidence and stakeholder changes; welfare loss ½×(5−4)×2=1 under stated assumptions.
- **2.7.subsidy_diagram** — Per-unit producer subsidy s=2 shifts supply to P=Q−2; buyer price 4, seller receipt 6, Q=6, public expenditure 12 and wedge 2. Build, compare stakeholder effects and financing; private-market overproduction loss is ½×(6−5)×2=1 when no externality and subsidy has no other benefits.
- **2.8.social_optimum_diagram** — MSB=10−Q and MSC=Q; shade social surplus between curves through Q*=5, label axes, crossing and total=25. Predict whether another unit adds net benefit at Q=4 versus Q=6; construct marginal net-benefit areas and explain optimum conditional on curves capturing all effects.
- **2.8.negative_production_diagram** — MPB=MSB=10−Q, MPC=Q, MSC=Q+2. Build market Qm=5 and social Q*=4, shade triangle between MSC and MSB from 4 to 5, area 1. External cost is MSC−MPC=2, overproduction; do not shift benefit curve.
- **2.8.negative_consumption_diagram** — MPB=10−Q, MSB=8−Q, MPC=MSC=Q. Build Qm=5, Q*=4, loss triangle area 1 between MSC and MSB over 4–5; marginal external damage is MPB−MSB=2. Predict overconsumption; do not describe it as a production-cost shift.
- **2.8.positive_production_diagram** — MPB=MSB=10−Q, MPC=Q+2, MSC=Q. Build Qm=4, Q*=5, shade loss between MSB and MSC from 4 to 5, area 1; external benefit is MPC−MSC=2. Predict underproduction and explain why social cost is below private cost.
- **2.8.positive_consumption_diagram** — MPB=8−Q, MSB=10−Q, MPC=MSC=Q. Build Qm=4, Q*=5, loss triangle area 1 between MSB and MSC, 4–5; MSB−MPB=2 and consumption is below social optimum. Practice contrasting with positive production.
- **2.8.pigouvian_diagram** — Reuse negative-production curves; add unit tax equal to marginal external cost at Q*=4, so effective private supply MPC+2=MSC, Q 5→4. Label tax wedge, buyer/seller prices, revenue 8 and eliminated loss triangle 1. Explain the outcome depends on accurately measured marginal damage and full compliance; a generic tax is not automatically optimal.
- **2.8.carbon_diagram** — Explicit polluting industry's price/output diagram with MPC, MSC, demand and a per-unit carbon tax, not an economy-wide emissions claim. Using preceding example show Q 5→4 and price 5→6, sellers receive 4; compare output and identify tax revenue 8. Actual emissions depend on emissions intensity and substitution, and taxes may not equal damage.
- **2.8.subsidy_diagram** — Explicit producer subsidy for positive production: use MPC=Q+2, MSC=Q, MSB=10−Q and subsidy 2, effective supply=MSC. Build Q 4→5, buyer price 5, seller receipt 7, expenditure 10; welfare triangle removed has area 1 before subsidy financing costs. A consumer subsidy for positive consumption is a distinct valid variant with a demand-side wedge.
- **2.8.regulation_diagram** — A chosen enforceable quantity cap in negative production, Qcap=Q*=4; mark market Qm=5 and welfare triangle 1 removed assuming compliance and same production technology. Compare a weaker cap Q=4.5 (remaining loss ½×0.5×1=0.25). This is one regulatory mechanism; standards may instead change costs/technology and need not produce a universal shift.
- **2.8.education_diagram** — A chosen information intervention for positive consumption: MPB shifts from 8−Q to 10−Q as perceived private benefit rises, assuming MSB fixed and effective learning. Build Q 4→5 and compare triangle 1 removed; show initially separate MPB/MSB and explain that actual response can be partial, absent or may change underlying preferences and social benefit. Do not assert every education campaign shifts demand by the full external benefit.

## Firm and market power (all HL)

Axes are price/cost/revenue per unit vertically and output per period horizontally. In all profit diagrams, identify Q where MR=MC with MC crossing MR from below, read price from demand/AR, and compare P with AC at that Q. Profit rectangle is (P−AC)×Q; negative gives loss. A loss-making firm can continue short run only if revenue covers variable cost; if AVC is omitted, explicitly assume P≥AVC and label continuation conditional. Long-run entry/exit requires stated assumptions. These are illustrative configurations rather than universal empirical outcomes.

- **2.11.perfect_competition_diagram** — Market D/S sets P and a separate small firm's horizontal P=D=AR=MR; add MC crossing. For market P=6, firm MR=6 and MC=2+Q, Q=4. Predict why a price taker cannot set its own price; build both panels and link the same P.
- **2.11.perfect_profit_diagram** — Three short-run firm panels share horizontal P=MR and rising MC; at Q=4 use P=6 and AC=4,6,8 respectively: profit 8, zero economic profit, loss 8. Layer AC and rectangles; for loss assume AVC≤6. Explain normal profit as included in AC, not zero accounting profit.
- **2.11.perfect_equilibrium_diagram** — Long-run competitive firm with P=MR=MC=min AC and P=MC, paired with market equilibrium. Example P=6, Q=4, MC intersects AC at minimum 6; show no profit rectangle and allocative efficiency given no externalities. Build curves and explain why P=MC corresponds to MB=MC under assumptions.
- **2.11.monopoly_diagram** — Downward AR/D, MR below AR, MC and AC, with abnormal, normal and loss cases. For D=10−Q, MR=10−2Q, MC=2, Q=4, P=6; AC at 4 is 4,6,8 respectively, giving 8,0,−8. Build MR=MC then price and AC rectangles; assume viable short-run loss where P≥AVC.
- **2.11.monopoly_welfare_diagram** — D=10−Q, MR=10−2Q, MC=2. Compare monopoly (Qm=4,Pm=6) with competitive benchmark (Qc=8,Pc=2) using the same demand and cost assumptions; shade DWL between demand and MC over 4–8: ½×4×4=8. This is a benchmark, not proof real competitive costs equal monopoly costs.
- **2.11.natural_monopoly_diagram** — Show D, MR, declining LRAC over relevant output and MC below LRAC; demand intersects declining LRAC. Example LRAC=2+12/Q, MC=2 and D=10−Q: monopoly Q=4,P=6, LRAC=5; at P=MC, Q=8 and LRAC=3.5, so loss=12 absent subsidy. Build layers and compare cost recovery; falling AC is a standard illustration of scale economies, not a general proof of cost subadditivity for every technology.
- **2.11.oligopoly_diagram** — Label an explicit joint-profit-maximizing collusive cartel as monopoly-like, D/MR/MC and joint Q/P; same D=10−Q, MR=10−2Q, MC=2 gives Q=4,P=6. Build aggregate not individual firm curves; discuss cheating and stability separately, without representing all oligopoly by this model.
- **2.11.monopolistic_profit_diagram** — Downward AR and MR below, MC and AC; three short-run outcomes, plus long-run zero-profit tangency. Use monopoly-example short-run values AC=4,6,8 at Q=4 with conditional loss operation; long-run AR tangent to AC at MR=MC, P=AC, typically Q below minimum AC. Construct each, explain entry shifts individual firm demand under appropriate assumptions, and check tangency rather than just an intersection.
- **2.11.monopolistic_demand_diagram** — Compare two downward demand curves through a common P/Q point, with monopolistic competition more elastic there under stated substitutability assumptions. Example at (Q,P)=(4,6), monopoly D=10−Q and differentiated-firm D=8−0.5Q: point PED magnitudes 1.5 and 3. Build and compare at same point and axes; this is a teaching assumption, not a universal ranking.
- **2.12.circular_flow_diagram** — Extend five-sector flow to at least two household groups with unequal factor ownership and factor-income arrows; firms, taxes, transfers and finance remain legible. Build unequal income then trace a possible saving/asset ownership feedback; illustrative group incomes 80/20 total 100 and savings 20/2 demonstrate divergent asset accumulation, not a claim this is the sole cause. Distinguish income from wealth and avoid assuming all saving becomes own investment automatically.

## Source checks and open decisions

The guide and scope evidence is in [curriculum research](research/curriculum.md) and [source validation](research/source-validation.md); those documents identify the 2022 guide's publication and scope limits. The following primary textbook sources support economic checks, not IB assessment scope: [OpenStax elasticity polar cases](https://openstax.org/books/principles-microeconomics-2e/pages/5-2-polar-cases-of-elasticity-and-constant-elasticity) distinguishes constant unit elasticity and curve shapes; [OpenStax elasticity and pricing](https://openstax.org/books/principles-economics-3e/pages/5-3-elasticity-and-pricing) supports the revenue relationship; [OpenStax firm output decisions](https://openstax.org/books/principles-microeconomics-2e/pages/8-2-how-perfectly-competitive-firms-make-output-decisions) supports the shutdown qualification; [OpenStax natural monopoly](https://openstax.org/books/principles-economics-3e/pages/9-1-how-monopolies-form-barriers-to-entry) and [regulation](https://openstax.org/books/principles-microeconomics-2e/pages/11-3-regulating-natural-monopolies) support the declining-cost illustration and regulatory trade-off; [OpenStax competitive markets](https://openstax.org/books/principles-microeconomics-2e/pages/3-key-concepts-and-summary) supports controls and surplus. [OpenStax externality definitions](https://openstax.org/books/principles-economics-3e/pages/12-key-terms) and [external benefits](https://openstax.org/books/principles-economics-3e/pages/13-key-terms) distinguish private and social effects; [OpenStax circular flows](https://openstax.org/books/introduction-business-2e/pages/1-3-how-business-and-economics-work) supports the two-sector flow directions, while the guide supplies the five-sector scope. All numbers above are original constructed examples, not copied figures.

Before implementation decide how construction is assessed (ordered layer choices versus other accessible actions), how much bilingual terminology and alternative text is required for each layer, and how to handle screen sizes without losing labels. The audit does not choose a particular renderer or promise universal policy effects. Verify all 40 IDs against rendered, keyboard-accessible construction and independent economic checks before changing coverage status; numerical checks alone do not prove presentation or accessibility.
