## BLOCKING A1 — finite initial-value PED does not support the stated revenue rule

Evidence: `assets/js/content-en.js:912` states that an inelastic response means a price rise raises revenue. The same claim appears in `assets/js/content-ja.js:698`, `assets/js/content-ko.js:698`, and `assets/js/content-zh-hant-hk.js:698`. The calculator explicitly uses initial-value percentage changes (`assets/js/models.js:9-10`), not point elasticity or the midpoint method.

Reproduction: `ECON_MODELS.ped(10,1,1,2).elasticity` returns `-0.9`, so the lesson classifies the response as inelastic. Yet revenue falls from `1×10=10` to `2×1=2`. These inputs are all within the actual controls' ranges (`assets/js/app.js:38`). Even the model's default `ped(10,8,10,12)` gives `-1` while revenue falls from 100 to 96. The subsequent reference to revenue being unchanged “locally” (`assets/js/content-en.js:913`) does not explain why the displayed finite-change classification cannot establish that result.

Smallest fix: retain the approved initial-value convention, but explicitly distinguish point/local elasticity's standard revenue relationship from finite initial-value estimates in this section, in all four locales. Tell learners to compare `P0×Q0` with `P1×Q1` for these finite examples, and give one counterexample or the exact factor `(1+ΔP/P0)(1+ΔQ/Q0)`. Do not replace the approved convention silently. Add a focused content/acceptance check for the caveat and verify the counterexample arithmetic; existing model tests correctly establish the formula but cannot detect this explanatory mismatch.
